# Guitar Practice System

> [!abstract] Practice is a weekly experiment, not a tour through a library. Keep one technical problem, one vocabulary set, and one musical application active long enough to produce audible evidence.

## What belongs in a block

Each four-week block has three linked objects:

1. **Vocabulary** — a scale, arpeggio, lick family, or harmonic device that
   explains where the notes come from.
2. **Mechanism** — one picking, fretting, timing, or coordination problem that
   can be isolated and measured.
3. **Music** — a transcription, tune fragment, or constrained improvisation in
   which the vocabulary and mechanism are actually heard.

Material that cannot yet fill one of those roles stays in the
[[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Practice/Practice Material Library|Practice Material Library]]. It does not enter the daily session merely because it is
interesting.

## Genre allocation

- **70% bluegrass and country** — lead vocabulary, rhythm, improvisation, and
  traditional repertoire.
- **20% jazz or classical cross-training** — alternate blocks, not a second
  simultaneous curriculum. Jazz develops chord-tone hearing and voice leading;
  classical work develops control, independence, articulation, and reading.
- **10% free play** — exploration, arranging, or following curiosity without a
  performance target.

This is a directional ratio across a month, not a timer that must be enforced
inside every session.

## The 45-minute session

| Stage | Time | Required output |
|---|---:|---|
| Set the target | 2m | One sentence: material, measure or pattern, tempo, and quality criterion |
| Prepare the motion | 5m | A relaxed, easy version of the week's picking or fretting motion |
| Build vocabulary | 8m | One pattern from memory, then one variation or connection |
| Train the mechanism | 10m | Short loops with a clean-repetition gate |
| Apply it to music | 15m | A named phrase plus one improvised or recomposed use |
| Record and review | 5m | One representative take and one next-session instruction |

### Speed rule

Use tempo as evidence, not as the purpose of the exercise.

1. Find a tempo at which the motion is relaxed and the subdivision is even.
2. Play three clean repetitions before increasing by 2–5 BPM.
3. After a miss, repeat once at the same tempo. After a second miss, reduce the
   tempo or shorten the loop.
4. Occasionally test a short burst above the working tempo to discover whether
   a different efficient motion appears; do not grind a tense motion faster.
5. Stop on pain, numbness, or persistent tension.

The log records **clean working tempo**, not the fastest accidental pass.

## Weekly rhythm

- **Sessions 1–2: decode.** Name intervals/chord tones, establish fingering and
  picking, and learn the smallest phrase accurately.
- **Sessions 3–4: connect.** Join positions or phrases and move through the
  relevant chord change.
- **Sessions 5–6: vary.** Change the ending, rhythm, register, or target chord
  tone while preserving the source phrase's function.
- **Session 7: prove.** Record the passage and an improvised chorus without
  stopping. Write the next block decision from the recording.

Missing a day does not restart the week. Resume at the next session type.

## Fifteen-minute floor

- 2m target and relaxed motion
- 4m vocabulary
- 6m current musical phrase
- 3m one recorded take and next instruction

A short session maintains continuity; it does not justify adding more material.

## Promotion and retirement

Promote material from the library into a block only when it has a clear
technical or musical job. At the end of a block:

- **retain** it as repertoire if it can be played from memory and recovered
  after a week away;
- **recycle** its best two to four phrases into vocabulary if the whole piece is
  no longer useful;
- **retire** it if it merely duplicates a stronger lesson or creates browsing
  without practice.
