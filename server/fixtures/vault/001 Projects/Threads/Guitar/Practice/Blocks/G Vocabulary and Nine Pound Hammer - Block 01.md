# Current Four-Week Block — G Vocabulary and Nine Pound Hammer

> [!goal] Convert G major-blues and minor-blues positions into connected bluegrass vocabulary, then hear and reuse that vocabulary inside Tony Rice's second solo on “Nine Pound Hammer.”

## Anchor material

- [[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Practice/Materials/Aaron Jaxon - Blues Scales for Bluegrass Guitar.pdf|Aaron Jaxon — Blues Scales for Bluegrass Guitar]] — five G major-blues positions, the first position an octave higher, and five G minor-blues positions with alternate-picking directions.
- [[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Practice/Materials/Mirek Hulan - Tony Rice Nine Pound Hammer Second Solo.pdf|Mirek Hulan — Tony Rice, Nine Pound Hammer second solo]] — 32-bar transcription in G shapes, capo 2, marked at quarter note = 240.
- [[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Tabs/G Major Pentatonic - 3 note patterns.tg|G Major Pentatonic — 3-note patterns]] — 8-measure sequence study.
- [[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Tabs/G Major Pentatonic - 4 note patterns.tg|G Major Pentatonic — 4-note patterns]] — 8-measure sequence study.
- [[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Tabs/Major Pentatonic - Pentatonic Switching Exercise.tg|Major Pentatonic — position-switching exercise]] — 15 measures connecting G, C, and D material.

The printed 240 BPM is a reference tempo, not the starting tempo. Begin wherever
the sixteenth-note figures remain even and the slides, pull-offs, and open-string
crossings speak clearly.

## Week 1 — map the home position and decode the solo

**Vocabulary:** G major-blues pattern 1 and G minor-blues pattern 1. Say the
scale degrees of the characteristic notes: major 3rd, minor 3rd, 4th, flat 5th,
5th, major 6th, and flat 7th.

**Mechanism:** alternate-picked one-octave fragments on two adjacent strings.
Use the 3-note pattern TuxGuitar file only as a sequencing device; keep the
accent on the first note of each three-note cell while the pick direction keeps
alternating.

**Music:** Nine Pound Hammer bars 1–8. First clap or speak the rhythm, then learn
two bars at a time. Circle notes that belong to the G major-blues collection and
mark where the line answers C or D instead of merely staying in a G shape.

**Improvisation constraint:** over G–C–G–D, use only pattern 1 and leave at
least one full beat of space in every bar.

## Week 2 — sequence without sounding like an exercise

**Vocabulary:** add G major-blues pattern 2 and connect it to pattern 1. Learn
one route upward and a different route downward.

**Mechanism:** alternate picking across the string changes in the 3-note and
4-note TuxGuitar patterns. Move one four-note cell through the scale, then alter
its rhythm so it stops sounding like a literal sequence.

**Music:** Nine Pound Hammer bars 9–16. Isolate the open-string crossings and
the move into the high-register repeated-note figure. Retain the articulation;
do not replace every slur with picking just to make the motion uniform.

**Improvisation constraint:** make two-bar phrases. Phrase one ends on a chord
tone of C; phrase two resolves to G.

## Week 3 — change positions with the harmony

**Vocabulary:** use the position-switching TuxGuitar exercise through G, C,
and D. Identify each landing note as root, 3rd, or 5th of the arriving chord.

**Mechanism:** short speed bursts on one difficult two-beat fragment from the
solo, followed by a full relaxed pass below working tempo. Film the picking hand
once if the string change feels trapped or bouncy.

**Music:** Nine Pound Hammer bars 17–24. Treat the high-register C phrase as a
separate technical study, then reconnect it to the descent and the G–D–G close.

**Improvisation constraint:** change position only when the chord changes. Land
on the 3rd of C or D on beat one at least once per chorus.

## Week 4 — turn transcription into vocabulary

**Vocabulary:** choose three cells from the solo: one G sound, one C response,
and one D-to-G resolution. Write the scale degrees and make one rhythmic or
ending variation of each.

**Mechanism:** full-solo endurance at a tempo that preserves tone and time.
Increase only after a complete clean pass; return to short loops for failures.

**Music:** bars 25–32, then the complete solo. Record one melody-faithful pass
and one chorus that combines the three extracted cells with original space.

**Improvisation constraint:** quote each extracted cell once, but never in the
same bar where it appears in the transcription.

## Completion test

- Play the complete solo from memory or from a one-page cue map without stopping.
- Play G major-blues pattern 1 into pattern 2 in both directions.
- Improvise one G–C–D chorus whose chord changes remain audible without backing.
- Record the clean working tempo and one specific remaining failure point.
- Choose the next block from the queue in the
  [[001 Projects/Threads/Guitar/Practice/Practice Material Library|Practice Material Library]].

## Optional cross-training day

If a seventh session is available, spend it on **one** of these, not both:

- jazz: one-octave Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 arpeggios, resolving to the 3rd of each new chord;
- classical: the top and bass voices of Bach's Bourrée, BWV 996, practiced
  separately for connection and independence.
